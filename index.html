Physics-informed neural networks for shock wave / boundary layer interaction

Shock wave / boundary layer interaction (SWBLI) sets the performance limit of supersonic inlets, transonic wings and over-expanded nozzles, and it is exactly the kind of flow that data-driven models handle badly: a discontinuity sitting on top of a viscous, frequently separated near-wall region. This project trains physics-informed neural networks on the compressible Navier–Stokes residual to reconstruct interaction regions from sparse measurements, and treats the failure modes as the actual subject — spectral bias that smears the shock, loss-term imbalance between residual and boundary conditions, and the way a separation bubble quietly disappears when the optimiser finds a smoother answer that scores well.

Current directions: adaptive loss weighting, domain decomposition around the shock foot, and quantitative comparison of the predicted separation length and wall pressure distribution against reference CFD rather than against a plotted field that merely looks correct.

In-house compressible CFD solver

A finite-volume solver for the compressible Euler and Navier–Stokes equations, written from the ground up rather than assembled from a framework: approximate Riemann solvers (Roe, HLLC), MUSCL reconstruction with slope limiters, explicit and implicit time integration, and boundary treatments that survive supersonic outflow.

Verification is part of the deliverable, not an afterthought — Sod's shock tube, supersonic wedge and oblique shock reflection, flat-plate laminar boundary layer against the Blasius solution, and grid convergence studies for observed order of accuracy. Writing the solver serves everything else on this page: it generates the reference fields the neural network work is trained and judged against, and it keeps the numerics inspectable instead of hidden behind a commercial black box.

CST parameterisation and aerodynamic shape optimisation

Class–Shape Transformation describes an aerofoil with a handful of Bernstein coefficients, which makes it a natural design vector: smooth by construction, compact enough for a surrogate model, and expressive enough to recover standard sections to within manufacturing tolerance. This project couples CST geometry to an aerodynamic evaluation — panel method for cheap sweeps, the in-house solver where compressibility matters — inside gradient-free and surrogate-assisted optimisation loops.

The interesting questions are not in the optimiser: how many coefficients before the design space stops being useful, how to keep geometric constraints (thickness, trailing edge, curvature) from being satisfied on paper but violated in practice, and how much of a reported drag reduction survives a mesh refinement.

Pitot-static probe design and calibration

An instrument is a model with a machining tolerance. This work covers the design, manufacture and calibration of Pitot and Pitot-static probes for laboratory use: head geometry, static port placement and count, internal cavity volume and its effect on response time, and the sensitivity of the reading to yaw and pitch misalignment.

CFD is used ahead of manufacture to predict the error envelope — where the static port sits relative to the head's own pressure recovery, how far off-axis the probe stays within an acceptable band — and wind tunnel calibration is used to find out how much of that prediction was true.

Focus areas

The projects above sit on a small set of recurring interests. The connecting thread is compressible flow that resists cheap modelling, and the honest evaluation of whatever tool — numerical, data-driven or experimental — is pointed at it.

High-speed aerodynamics

Transonic and supersonic flow, shock structure, shock-induced separation, and the interaction between a discontinuity and a viscous wall layer.

Scientific machine learning

Physics-informed neural networks and PDE-constrained training, with attention to where they break: sharp gradients, stiff loss landscapes, unverifiable smoothness.

Numerical methods

Finite volume discretisation, approximate Riemann solvers, high-resolution reconstruction, time integration, and verification against analytical benchmarks.

Shape optimisation

CST and related parameterisations, design-space dimensionality, constraint handling, and surrogate-assisted search for aerodynamic shape design.

Experimental aerodynamics

Probe design and calibration, pressure measurement, uncertainty budgets, and wind tunnel work that puts a number on how wrong the simulation was.

Surrogate & reduced-order models

Cheap approximations of expensive solvers — regression surrogates, modal decomposition — and the question of when their extrapolation stops being trustworthy.
